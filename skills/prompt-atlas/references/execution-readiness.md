# Execution readiness

Use this reference only when the contract must drive direct or autonomous work. Intent Take owns protection selection and the semantic commitments carried in the contract; the selected carrier or harness owns concrete commands, files, enforcement, and executor topology.

## Readiness envelope

- **Reality and baseline** — carry the verified target state, relevant gates, false-green risks, available verifier, and task invariants. Carry a measured baseline and source when completion compares, preserves, migrates, regresses, counts, or claims performance; otherwise carry the first safe check and what a mismatch blocks.
- **Write boundary** — carry verified writable paths or owned seams when multiple edit surfaces, shared state, unrelated changes, or likely scope drift exist; preserve everything else.
- **Autonomy** — select for unattended work. Carry every necessary reversible default with evidence state and cost of error; surface user-owned direction changes before execution.
- **Continuity** — select when work may outlive one context window. Carry the resumable objective, completed evidence, next action, and unresolved dependency; the carrier chooses the storage protocol.
- **Parallel safety** — only with user authorization. Carry non-overlapping writes, an owned integration seam, and post-integration proof.

## Protection-selection grammar

Apply these decisions in order:

1. **Proof** — every execution or correctness claim needs the smallest evidence that directly exercises the intended outcome: a target test, replay or trace, contract example, known input/output, runtime observation, or reproducible manual check. A build alone proves only that the code builds. Carry a verified gate when available; otherwise carry the proof obligation as work and completion evidence.
2. **Reverse validation** — require a known-failure or red-to-green check when the gate is new, changed, proxy-only, silent on failure, or could pass without the target behavior. This proves the gate can distinguish right from wrong.
3. **Integrity** — require an integrity boundary when the executor can alter the gate, evidence, baseline, threshold, sample, fixture, assertion, mock, or another cheaper proxy for success. Freeze the relevant judging rule and name only plausible shortcuts; the carrier owns enforcement.
4. **Stop-loss and recovery** — require a failure boundary when retries can waste substantial effort or an attempt can degrade, destroy, or irreversibly change a verified state. Carry the stop threshold, last acceptable baseline, recovery expectation, and honest blocked outcome.
5. **Independent evaluation** — disallow self-attested completion when proof is indirect, remains gameable after Integrity, the task is high-risk or behavior-preserving, the executor designed both proof and implementation, or completion depends on overall user value rather than a technical gate. Carry what an independent verifier must judge; the carrier chooses its protocol.

Independent evaluation may be omitted only when Proof directly exercises the target behavior, reverse validation proves the gate is live, Integrity freezes plausible cheat paths, and risk is low and scope narrow.

## Placement grammar

- **Current Context** — known gates, false-green risks, baselines, and available verifier.
- **Boundary** — frozen baselines and judging rules, write scope, defaults, and prohibited shortcuts.
- **Immediate Task** — proof work, reverse validation, continuity, and artifacts needed by an external verifier.
- **Success / Stop** — required evidence, proof that the gate distinguishes right from wrong, stop and recovery conditions, and any pending independent-verification gate.

Execution closes on achieved behavior with proportional trusted evidence; exploration closes on a bounded decision or finding. Never invent a command or number, and never accept a degraded or self-attested result when the selected grammar forbids it.
